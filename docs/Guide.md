# Using Mint

## Your balance

Your balance is the amount you entered on first launch, plus everything paid or received since.

When Mint and your bank disagree, choose **Adjust…** in Settings (or **File → Adjust Balance…**, ⇧⌘B) and enter what your bank shows for any day up to today. The balance then counts from that amount and day:

- Entries paid on or before that day are treated as part of the amount.
- Entries paid later, and scheduled ones, still change the balance.
- Earlier entries still count in Statistics.
- Choosing an earlier day replaces balances set on or after it. Mint warns you first, and ⌘Z undoes it.

Adjustments show in Transactions as **Manual adjustment**, and don't count as income or spending.

## Entries

| Kind       | Effect on balance |
|------------|-------------------|
| Spend      | Takes money out   |
| Income     | Adds money        |
| Investment | Takes money out, into your investments |
| Subsidy    | Adds money, tracked apart from income |

Each entry has a status, shown at the right of its row:

| Mark | Status | Meaning |
|------|--------|---------|
| Red label | **Unpaid** | No money has moved yet. Give it a due date to see it in Coming up, marked **Due soon** the week before and **Overdue** after. |
| Amber clock | **Scheduled** | Counts as paid on its date. Marked **Late** if that's after the due date. |
| Green check | **Paid** | The money has moved. |

Income and subsidies say *received* instead of *paid*; investments say *invested*.

Click an entry to edit it, click its status mark to change the status and dates, or right-click for **Mark as Paid Today**, **Duplicate** and **Delete**.

**Repeating entries** (rent, paychecks, subscriptions) can repeat weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly, every 3 months or yearly, up to a year ahead. Editing or deleting one asks whether to change just that one or the ones after it too. Repeats already paid or due are left alone.

## Screens

- **Overview:** your balance, where it'll be in four weeks, its lowest point ahead, and a chart from a month ago to three months out, dotted where it's projected and red below your low-balance limit. Hover over the chart to see the balance on any day, compared with today's. Below: **Overdue** entries, **Coming up** in the next four weeks with your balance after each, and **This month**.
- **Transactions:** everything in one list: overdue, coming up, undated, then past months. Filter by kind at the top; adjustments show under **All**.
- **Statistics:** totals by kind, net, and spending by category for this month, this year or all time, plus money in and out for the last 12 months.

Search (⌘F) finds entries by name, category or notes. Press Return to move into the results.

## Keyboard

Everything works from the keyboard. **Help → Keyboard Shortcuts** (⌘?) lists every shortcut. The main ones:

| Keys | Action |
|------|--------|
| ⌘N | New entry |
| ⌘1–⌘3, ⇧⌘] / ⇧⌘[ | Switch screens |
| ↑ ↓, ⌘↑ ⌘↓ | Move between entries, or jump to the first and last |
| Return | Edit the selected entry |
| Space | Change its status |
| ⇧⌘C | Mark it paid |
| ⌘D, Delete | Duplicate, delete |
| ← → | Switch the Transactions filter or Statistics period |
| ⌘1–⌘4 (in the editor) | Pick the kind |

## Reminders

Mint sends a notification before anything unpaid is due (a day ahead unless you change it), and the day after if it's still unpaid. Click one to open the entry, or use its **Mark Paid** button. It also warns you before your balance is expected to drop below your limit ($0 by default). Notifications arrive even when Mint isn't open; macOS asks for permission the first time.

## Settings

Settings (⌘,) shows your balance with **Adjust…**, and under **History** your starting balance and every adjustment, so you can delete a mistake. It also turns each kind of reminder on or off, sets the low-balance limit and when reminders arrive: how many days ahead, and at what time. **Reset…** erases everything and can't be undone.
